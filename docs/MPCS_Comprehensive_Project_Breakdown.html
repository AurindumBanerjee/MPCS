Executive Abstract & Pedagogical Purpose

Human intelligence is defined not solely by uniform processing power, but by deciding how much cognitive processing a situation actually demands. When a stimulus threatens immediate survival, high-latency deliberative reasoning is fatal; when an ambiguous situation arises with subtle emotional undertones, reflexive heuristics cause catastrophic errors. This document serves as the definitive architectural compendium for the Malleable Perceptual-Cognitive System (MPCS) and its large-scale medical manifestation, LocalKG.

Unlike high-level project summaries, this document builds every concept from first principles: every theoretical term is defined in plain language, every mathematical formula is broken down into its variables with step-by-step worked examples, and all five engineering pipelines are traced from raw inputs, through intermediate transformations, to final verified outputs.

SECTION 00

Comprehensive Primer & Term Glossary (From Absolute Basics)

Before examining the mathematics and architectures, we establish rigorous, intuitive definitions for every core term used throughout the repository.

1. Percept Cognitive Psychology
A percept is the meaningful mental representation formed after sensory receptors collect raw physical signals. While a "sensation" is raw light hitting retina or vibrations hitting eardrums, a "percept" is the structured psychological object: e.g., [Red, Circular, High-Pitch Screech, Hot Surface, Threat Context]. In MPCS, a percept bundles sensory channels, emotional arousal, and contextual metadata into a single computable object.
Role in MPCS: The fundamental unit of input fed into the salience engine and stored in experiential memory.
2. Salience Neurobiology
Salience is the state or quality by which an item stands out relative to its neighbors. In biological brains, the "Salience Network" (anchored in the anterior insula and dorsal anterior cingulate cortex) acts as an attentional switchboard: high-salience stimuli (a sudden tiger roar, excruciating burn) immediately hijack conscious processing, while low-salience stimuli (background humming) are delegated to subconscious background tracking.
Role in MPCS: The scalar value calculated by our 4-factor equation that decides whether to trigger a reflex or allow deliberation.
3. Dual-Process Theory (System 1 vs System 2) Cognitive Science
Formulated by psychologists (Wason, Evans, Kahneman), this theory posits two distinct modes of human thought:
  • System 1 (Fast / Reflexive): Operates automatically, intuitively, and without voluntary control. Minimal energy, latency < 5 ms. E.g., swerving away from a falling rock.
  • System 2 (Slow / Deliberative): Allocates attention to effortful mental operations, logical deduction, memory borrowing, and counterfactual planning. High energy, latency > 200 ms. E.g., solving 17 × 24.
Role in MPCS: The primary routing architecture of our cognitive engine.
4. Somatic Markers Neuroscience (Damasio)
Proposed by Antonio Damasio, somatic markers are physiological ("bodily") emotional reactions—such as an elevated heart rate, visceral gut tension, or skin conductance changes—associated with past experiences. When facing an unfamiliar or complex decision, somatic markers instantly bias choices by attaching visceral "danger" or "incentive" signals to options before cognitive reasoning begins.
Role in MPCS: Formally represented by the continuous Valence and Arousal signals that modulate memory retrieval.
5. Bloom Filter & Counting Bloom Filter Computer Science
A Bloom Filter is a space-efficient probabilistic data structure used to test set membership. Using an array of $m$ bits and $k$ independent hash functions, it can return either "Definitely not in set" (100% certainty, zero false negatives) or "Possibly in set" (with a known false-positive probability $p$). A d-left Counting Bloom Filter (dlCBF) replaces single bits with multi-bit counters split into $d$ sub-tables, allowing items to be deleted and modified over time.
Role in MPCS: Tested in Generation G2 & G3 for O(1) novelty detection (and produced a crucial negative result).
6. Z-Numbers & Z*-Numbers Fuzzy Logic & Uncertainty
Introduced by Lotfi Zadeh, a Z-number $Z = (A, B)$ represents a real-world assertion with uncertainty, where $A$ is a fuzzy restriction on values (e.g. "temperature is hot") and $B$ is a measure of certainty or reliability (e.g. "very likely", confidence = 0.85). In MPCS, a $Z^*$-number expands this to include temporal and affective dimensions: $\langle ext{Context}, ext{Time}, ext{Subject}, ext{Belief}, ext{Emotion} angle$.
Role in MPCS: Allows each sensory channel to carry an independent confidence weight rather than assuming perfect sensor reliability.
7. VAD Dimensional Affect Space Affective Computing
The Valence-Arousal-Dominance (VAD) model (Mehrabian & Russell, 1974; Warriner et al., 2013) maps all human emotional states into a 3D continuous Euclidean space normalized to $[0.0, 1.0]$:
  • Valence (Pleasure / Distress): How positive or negative the emotion is. 0.0 = extreme suffering/terror; 0.5 = neutral; 1.0 = ecstasy/joy.
  • Arousal (Activation / Urgency): Physiological alertness. 0.0 = coma/torpor/sleep; 0.5 = baseline calm; 1.0 = panic/rage/mania.
  • Dominance (Agency / Control): Sense of mastery. 0.0 = helpless/submissive/trapped; 1.0 = in-command/empowered.
Role in MPCS: Powers the affective grounding of symptoms and dynamically modulates spreading activation in the knowledge graph.
8. Homeopathic Proving & Materia Medica Historical Medical Epistemology
A proving (German: Prüfung, test) is a human toxicological and clinical protocol developed by Samuel Hahnemann (1796) where healthy volunteers systematically ingest serial dilutions of a substance and record every subjective psychological, somatic, and environmental change. A Materia Medica is the comprehensive encyclopedia documenting hundreds of medicines and their thousands of attested proving symptom rubrics.
Role in MPCS: Acts as our massive, real-world human variorum of subjective cognitive-affective experiences.
9. Modality (Aggravation & Amelioration) Clinical Phenomenology
In clinical phenomenology, a modality is an environmental, postural, temporal, weather, or physiological factor that alters the intensity of a symptom:
  • Aggravation (Worse / <): Factors that intensify suffering (e.g. Worse: Warmth of bed, Worse: Midnight to 2 AM, Worse: Touch or noise).
  • Amelioration (Better / >): Factors that bring relief (e.g. Better: Open cold air, Better: Lying on painful side, Better: Hot drinks).
Role in MPCS: Represented as explicit typed Modality nodes in the graph linked via AGGRAVATED BY and AMELIORATED BY edges.
10. Spreading Activation & Active Subgraph Pruning Associative Memory (ACT-R)
Spreading activation (Collins & Loftus, 1975) is the mechanism by which accessing one concept in memory automatically energizes connected semantic neighbors. Activation spreads outward through network links, decaying with distance and branch factor. Active Subgraph Pruning deactivates any concept whose energy fails to reach a threshold floor, leaving a compact, relevant sub-network for focused reasoning.
Role in MPCS: Powers the TIDE engine in Generation G9 and runs live inside our interactive web canvas.
11. HNSW vs BM25 vs Exact KNN Information Retrieval
Three distinct retrieval algorithms with complementary strengths:
  • Exact KNN: Compares query against 100% of stored vectors. Guarantees true best $k$ results ($O(N)$), serving as the absolute benchmark baseline.
  • HNSW (Hierarchical Navigable Small World): Multi-layer skip-list graph. Provides sub-linear ($O(\log N)$) approximate vector search for dense semantic concepts.
  • BM25 (Best Matching 25): Term-frequency inverse-document-frequency ranking. Catches rare, exact medical strings and Latin tokens that dense embeddings miss.
Role in MPCS: Formulates the tri-channel scoring engine ($w_t S_{ ext{text}} + w_a S_{ ext{affect}} + w_h S_{ ext{hierarchy}}$) planned in Meeting 5.
12. Passive Thread & Latency Budgeting Distributed Cognitive Architecture
When an urgent stimulus demands a decision before deep memory exploration can finish, latency budgeting enforces an absolute cutoff time (e.g. 15 ms). The agent acts immediately based on the best partial evidence gathered so far, while the incomplete graph search is offloaded to a background passive thread. Once complete, the passive thread records whether the rushed action was counterfactually verified by deep reasoning.
Role in MPCS: Solves the latency-accuracy trade-off in the TIDE engine.
SECTION 01

Philosophical & Cognitive Foundations

Modern artificial intelligence architectures typically fall into two extremes: either rigid expert rule bases with zero adaptability, or massive auto-regressive transformers that process every trivial token with maximum uniform latency. Human cognition operates under radically different constraints. MPCS synthesizes five foundational paradigms from cognitive science and AI history:

Marvin Minsky (1986, 2006)
Society of Mind & Emotion Machine
Mind is built from a society of simple, non-intelligent agents. There is no single centralized "thinking" module. Emotion is not an irrational interference, but a primary cognitive mechanism for switching modes of thinking and directing resource allocation under urgency.
Daniel Kahneman (2011)
Dual-Process Theory (System 1 & 2)
Cognitive systems maintain two distinct operating regimes: System 1 (fast, autonomous, parallel, heuristic-driven, low energy) and System 2 (slow, deliberative, sequential, rule-following, high energy). Intelligence lies in gating between them.
Antonio Damasio (1994)
Somatic Marker Hypothesis
Emotional processes guide (or bias) behavior, particularly decision-making. Bodily states (somatic markers) tag incoming percepts with visceral positive or negative valence, pruning dangerous branches before logical calculation begins.
Douglas Lenat (1984–present)
CYC & Microtheories
Formalizing common-sense knowledge requires structuring reality into first-order logical rules of thought, contextual microtheories, and explicit belief structures that can ground perceptual symbols into coherent semantic taxonomies.
Lotfi Zadeh & Dasgupta (2018)
Z*-Numbers & Neural Novelty
Real-world percepts inherently carry fuzziness and uncertainty. A Z*(S) number encodes <Context, Time, Subject, Belief, Emotion>, allowing memory retrieval to balance cognitive certainty against affective weight.
SECTION 02

The MPCS Cognitive Architecture & Decision Formulas

The core MPCS framework consists of an end-to-end perception-decision-learning loop. Sensory inputs are dynamically structured, scored for salience, routed to either a fast reflex path or a budgeted deliberative path, and continuously updated via experiential memory.

Figure 2.1 — End-to-End MPCS Perception, Salience, Routing, and Memory Loop
Formula 2.1 — Multi-Factor Salience Equation Decision Gating
Salience(S) = w₁·Intensity + w₂·Arousal + w₃·|Valence - 0.5|·2 + w₄·Novelty(S)
  • Intensity (I ∈ [0, 1]): Physical energy magnitude of stimulus (e.g. peak decibels, surface temperature, photon flux).
  • Arousal (A ∈ [0, 1]): Neurobiological activation level. Panic/alarm = 0.95; calm baseline = 0.10.
  • |Valence - 0.5|·2 ∈ [0, 1]: Affective extremity. Why absolute deviation from 0.5? Because both extreme terror ($V=0.05 \implies |0.05-0.5|\cdot 2 = 0.90$) and extreme reward ($V=0.95 \implies 0.90$) command urgency, while neutral stimuli ($V=0.50 \implies 0.00$) have zero affective salience.
  • Novelty(S) ∈ [0, 1]: Unfamiliarity gap between current percept $S$ and closest cluster in memory. Calculated via distance to $k$-nearest neighbors.
  • Weights (w₁ + w₂ + w₃ + w₄ = 1.0): Dynamically adapted feature importance weights.
Worked Example: Suppose an agent touches a scorching stove ($I=0.90$, $A=0.95$, $V=0.05 \implies ext{AffExt}=0.90$, $ ext{Novelty}=0.10$). With equal weights $w_i = 0.25$:
$ ext{Salience} = 0.25(0.90) + 0.25(0.95) + 0.25(0.90) + 0.25(0.10) = 0.225 + 0.2375 + 0.225 + 0.025 = \mathbf{0.7125}$.
If routing threshold $ heta = 0.60$, $ ext{Salience} (0.7125) > heta \implies$ Instant System 1 Reflex triggered (< 1 ms)!
Formula 2.2 — Dual-Path Routing & Temporal Memory Decay Decision & Memory
Route(S) = (System 1 Reflex) if Salience(S) > θ else (System 2 Deliberation) MemoryWeight(e) = BaseRelevance(e) × e^(-λ · Δt) × (1.0 + β · OutcomeReward(e))
  • θ (Theta Threshold): Dynamic gating boundary learned from environmental risk history.
  • e^(-λ · Δt): Exponential forgetting curve where $\Delta t$ is elapsed steps and $\lambda$ is temporal decay rate (older memories lose influence).
  • OutcomeReward(e): Positive or negative reinforcement observed when action was taken in episode $e$.
SECTION 03

Chronological Evolution of Experimental Generations (G1 to G9)

Rather than jumping straight to complex black-box neural networks, MPCS was developed through a disciplined sequence of nine distinct architectural generations. Each generation was built to isolate and test a specific cognitive capability or hypothesis:

Generation Name Primary Mechanism Added Key Finding / Why It Was Built
G1 Basic Model Global top-5 exact-match feature counting across vision + audio. Established initial baseline. Proved that unweighted feature counting fails when danger depends on subtle physical interactions (touch/heat).
G2 Bloom Filter Standard Bloom Filter gating memory scan for $O(1)$ novelty check. Negative result: Speedup was illusory for small banks; strict hashing caused 100% false rejections on near-misses, dropping accuracy by 15%.
G3 Z + dlCBF d-left Counting Bloom Filter + Z-numbers (confidence fixed at 1.0). Proved that Z-numbers at static confidence are mathematically identical to feature counting. Prompted the development of per-modality confidence in G4.
B2 Baseline v2 Expansion to 4 sensory channels (touch, smell) and 5 actions (+withdraw). Resolved sensory blindness to thermal and mechanical impact scenes; established the standard 4-channel perceptual baseline.
G4 Core v2 Per-modality confidence weighting and per-action top-5 voting. Crucial negative result: Independent per-action voting allowed weakly related high-reward memories to outvote exact matches, crashing accuracy to 24%.
G5 HyST (Hysteresis) Two-stage retrieval: Hard categorical constraint slot, then graded soft kernel. The Core Breakthrough: Solved G4's voting flaw by filtering out incompatible actions before averaging. Accuracy leaped from 24% to 100% on Bank v2.
G6 HMGI Hybrid Multimodal Graph Indexing with sensory modality partitions. Inspired by recipe graphs. Demonstrated that partition lookup overhead exceeded linear scans on small tabular banks, pointing to the need for large-scale corpora.
G7 SHMF Structured Hierarchical Memory Filtering with automatic relaxation. Gracefully relaxed hard constraints when no candidates met strict criteria. Highest accuracy (75.6%) on noisy, contradictory query sets.
G8 KERL Knowledge-Enhanced Retrieval Layer + Near-Miss Safety Guard. Introduced graph-term fusion combining topological knowledge with perceptual experience. Achieved the lowest unsafe error rate (1.7%).
G9 TIDE Timed Iterative Deliberation Engine with spreading activation. Added latency-budgeted deliberation, passive thread handoff, and ACT-R style spreading activation with active subgraph pruning.

Key Engineering Insight: The Rule of Low vs High Cardinality

The clearest theoretical result from this benchmark was that membership filters (Bloom / dlCBF) fail catastrophically in cognitive perception. In multi-modal perceptual spaces, exact identical feature configurations almost never repeat; hashing an entire percept closes the gate on exactly the near-matches needed for generalisation.

Instead, intelligence must screen on the low-cardinality structure of the world (e.g. categorical context, anatomical region, broad sensory modality) via hard constraint filtering, and then apply soft continuous kernels (embeddings, spreading activation, VAD affect) over the high-cardinality contents.

SECTION 05

The Paradigm Shift: From Synthetic Percepts to the Materia Medica Variorum

By Meeting 5, the synthetic agent experiments had verified the cognitive dynamics of HyST, SHMF, and TIDE. However, synthetic toy datasets suffered from an artificial ceiling: they lacked the messy semantic depth, intricate subjective symptom phenomenology, and emotional richness of real human experience.

This motivated the pivotal shift to classical Homeopathic Materia Medica (Samuel Hahnemann’s Materia Medica Pura Vol. 2 and William Boericke’s Pocket Manual).

Formula 5.1 — Tri-Channel Hybrid Information Retrieval Scoring Search & Memory
Score(Query, Record) = w_t · S_text(Q, R) + w_a · S_affect(Q, R) + w_h · S_hierarchy(Q, R)
  • S_text(Q, R) ∈ [0, 1]: Lexical and semantic match score. Computed as a convex combination of BM25 (exact clinical token match) and Dense Cosine Similarity (paraphrase match).
  • S_affect(Q, R) ∈ [0, 1]: Emotional alignment score in Euclidean VAD space: $1.0 - rac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \|\mathbf{VAD}_Q - \mathbf{VAD}_R\|_2$.
  • S_hierarchy(Q, R) ∈ [0, 1]: Shared taxonomic depth along the anatomical tree (Mind / Head / Eye / Throat / Stomach): $ rac{ ext{Shared Levels}}{\max( ext{Depth}_Q, ext{Depth}_R)}$.
Worked Example: Query = "throbbing violent headache worse noise" ($V=0.15, A=0.88, D=0.20$, Region=Head/Temples).
Candidate A matches on text ($S_t=0.85$), emotional panic ($S_a=0.92$), and anatomical region ($S_h=1.00$).
With weights $w_t=0.50, w_a=0.30, w_h=0.20$: $ ext{Score} = 0.50(0.85) + 0.30(0.92) + 0.20(1.00) = 0.425 + 0.276 + 0.200 = \mathbf{0.901}$ (Top Rank).
SECTION 06

Knowledge Graph Pipeline Engineering (LocalKG)

To build a production knowledge graph without external API costs, rate limits, or data leakage, we created LocalKG: an air-gapped pipeline executing over local Ollama LLMs and ChromaDB vector stores.

Step-by-Step Engineering Pipeline

01
Multi-PDF Ingestion & Typographical Markup Parser
INPUT: Raw PDF files (Boericke 1,069 pp, Hahnemann 742 pp) OUTPUT: Parsed JSONL with bold/italic Markdown spans
Extracts page spans via PyMuPDF (fitz). Checks font flags (bit 16 for bold, bit 2 for italic) to wrap terms in **bold** and _italic_. Employs OCR-tolerant heading regular expressions to handle classic typos (e.g. ARUICA. $ o$ Arnica montana; Ifose. $ o$ Nose).
02
Chunk Consolidation & Header Preservation
INPUT: Fragmented parsed page lines OUTPUT: Chunks JSONL (stable chunk_id, ~2,600 chars each)
Groups fragmented section paragraphs into rich, consolidated chunks. Prepends ### {Remedy}: {Section} headers so the LLM retains full anatomical context. Generates deterministic chunk identifiers (chunk:{source_id}:{ordinal}).
03
Air-Gapped Ollama LLM Extraction Engine
INPUT: Consolidated text chunk + Prompt extract_v1.md OUTPUT: Pydantic ChunkExtraction object
Dispatches structured queries to local qwen2.5:7b-instruct via localhost:11434. Enforces strict schema decoding using Pydantic JSON schemas via Ollama's format parameter. Checks DiskLLMCache first to eliminate duplicate computation. Caps parallelism with num_thread=6 and Windows below-normal process priority.
04
Deterministic Validation & Cognitive-Affective Enrichment
INPUT: Raw ChunkExtraction + Source Chunk Text OUTPUT: Validated, VAD-scored Occurrences list
Verifies quote faithfulness (verbatim substring check). Overrides LLM typography emphasis with ground-truth font markup in chunk text. Computes continuous VAD affect coordinates, adjective severity scores, composite regression weights, and maps mental symptoms to canonical Cognitive Domains.
05
Multi-Source Fusion & 60 FPS Hardware-Accelerated Canvas
INPUT: Extracted JSONL runs + docs/ PubMed clinical records OUTPUT: Standalone interactive HTML visualizer + .graph.json
Fuses historical provings with modern PubMed RCTs and MeSH descriptors. Resolves all remedy abbreviations into canonical Latin hubs. Calculates structured Galaxy Constellation and Radial Mandala coordinates. Executes spatial-grid collision relaxation and renders 1,000+ nodes at 60 FPS with in-browser TIDE spreading activation.
SECTION 07

Continuous Affective Metrics & Linear Regression Scoring

In accordance with meeting directives, the relationship between symptoms, typographical grades, and psychological intensity is formalized as a linear regression model:

Formula 7.1 — Continuous Linear Regression Composite Severity Weight Affective Weighting
W = 3.0 × [ β₁·(Grade / 3) + β₂·AdjSev + β₃·Arousal + β₄·Confidence ] where: β₁ = 0.40, β₂ = 0.30, β₃ = 0.20, β₄ = 0.10 (Σβ = 1.00)
  • Grade / 3 ∈ {0.333, 0.667, 1.000}: Normalized historical verification grade from repertory typography (Plain = 1, Italic = 2, Bold/CAPS = 3).
  • AdjSev ∈ [0.20, 1.00]: Adjective severity score extracted from SEVERITY_ADJECTIVES lexicon: $\min(1.0, 0.75 \cdot \max + 0.25 \cdot ext{mean})$.
  • Arousal ∈ [0.00, 1.00]: Neurobiological activation level derived from VAD lexicon.
  • Confidence ∈ [0.50, 1.00]: LLM extraction confidence score.
  • Scale Factor 3.0: Scales the resulting normalized score back into the classical $[1.0, 3.0]$ homeopathic continuum.
Worked Example: Symptom = "Great fear of death with tossing anguish".
Typography = Bold ($ ext{Grade}=3 \implies ext{Norm}=1.00$). Matched adjectives: great (0.80), anguish (0.92) $\implies ext{AdjSev} = 0.75(0.92) + 0.25(0.86) = 0.905$.
Lexicon Arousal = $0.90$ (panic). LLM Confidence = $1.00$.
$ ext{InnerSum} = 0.40(1.00) + 0.30(0.905) + 0.20(0.90) + 0.10(1.00) = 0.40 + 0.2715 + 0.180 + 0.100 = \mathbf{0.9515}$.
$ ext{Composite Weight } W = 3.0 imes 0.9515 = \mathbf{2.855} ext{ / } 3.00$ (reflecting extreme clinical urgency!).
Formula 7.2 — VAD Vector Pooling & Peak Arousal Formulation Psychometrics
Valence = (1 / |T|) · Σ V(t), Dominance = (1 / |T|) · Σ D(t) Arousal = max_{t ∈ T} A(t), Coverage = |Matched Tokens| / |Total Tokens|
  • Mean Valence & Dominance: Average hedonic tone and sense of agency across all attested words.
  • Peak Arousal (max): In cognitive emergency and clinical triage, a single panic-inducing cue (e.g. "fears death") governs biological salience, regardless of whether surrounding descriptive words are calm.

SECTION 09

Hardware-Accelerated Interactive Canvas & TIDE Simulation

In Section 6, the initial SVG DOM renderer experienced browser lockups when attempting to render 1,113 nodes and 2,002 links simultaneously ($O(N^2)$ physics loop = 111M operations). We re-engineered the visualization engine into a high-performance, 60 FPS HTML5 <canvas> architecture:

Formula 9.1 — Client-Side TIDE Spreading Activation & Pruning Graph Reasoning
SeedScore(s) = (Hits / TotalTerms) × (1.0 + 0.80·Arousal) × (Weight / 2.0) SpreadEnergy(v, hop+1) = max_{u ∈ N(v)} [ Activation(u) · γ / deg(u) ] PruneCondition: Activation(v) < 0.18 × max_{s ∈ Seeds} SeedScore(s)
  • SeedScore(s): Query matching score boosted by the node's intrinsic affective arousal. High-arousal symptoms (panic, delirium) act as stronger energetic sources.
  • γ (Hop Decay Factor = 0.58): Dissipation rate preventing infinite energy loops over cycles.
  • deg(u) (Degree Normalization): Divides energy across outgoing edges so high-degree hubs don't artificially amplify energy.
  • Prune Cutoff (0.18 · MaxSeed): Strict dynamic threshold that deactivates weak peripheral associations, displaying real-time kept/pruned counts and pruning ratio.

Future Roadmap

  • Evolutionary Salience Optimization: Dynamically learning the routing threshold $ heta$ and salience weights ($w_1, \dots, w_4$) through reinforcement learning across simulated clinical cases.
  • Persona & Constitution Models: Instantiating distinct synthetic patient personas (e.g. hyperaroused anxious profiles vs melancholic hypoaroused profiles) to evaluate divergent vs convergent remedy retrieval.
  • Continuous Multi-Agent Society of Mind: Implementing Marvin Minsky's agency architecture as interacting subagents (Perceptor, Saliency Critic, Associator, Memory Reflector) operating over the knowledge graph.
  • Real-Time Edge Inference: Porting the pruned subgraphs and local embedding index onto low-power edge accelerators for real-time robotic or bedside cognitive assistance.
