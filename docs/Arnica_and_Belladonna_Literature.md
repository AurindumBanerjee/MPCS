## 1. Arnica Montana (Leopard's Bane)

### Reference 1.1: Perioperative and Post-Traumatic Anti-Inflammatory Effects
* **Identifier**: `pmid:27912633` / `PMC5373408`
* **Title**: Perioperative Arnica montana for Reduction of Ecchymosis, Edema, and Pain in Facial Surgery: Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
* **Authors**: Iannitti T, Morales-Medina JC, Bellavite P, Rottigni V, Palmieri B.
* **Source**: *American Journal of Therapeutics*, 2016.
* **Type**: Clinical Systematic Review & Meta-analysis
* **Abstract**:
  Arnica montana has been widely applied as a natural anti-inflammatory agent for post-traumatic and postoperative swelling, bleeding, and pain management. We performed a systematic review evaluating controlled clinical trials of oral and topical Arnica preparations across surgical procedures (plastic, facial, orthopedic). Results indicate significant reductions in postoperative ecchymosis (bruising) and swelling compared to placebo or inactive controls in plastic and soft-tissue surgery, with low incidence of adverse effects. Its action is attributed in part to sesquiterpene lactones (including helenalin and dihydrohelenalin), which modulate NF-kappa-B activation and cytokine expression.

### Reference 1.2: Osteoarthritis and Musculoskeletal Pain (Topical Comparison)
* **Identifier**: `pmid:17405692`
* **Title**: Choosing between NSAID and arnica for topical treatment of hand osteoarthritis in a randomised, double-blind study
* **Authors**: Widrig R, Suter A, Saller R, Melzer J.
* **Source**: *Rheumatology International*, 2007.
* **Type**: Randomized Controlled Trial
* **Abstract**:
  To evaluate the efficacy and safety of topical Arnica montana gel in comparison with ibuprofen gel (5%) in patients with active osteoarthritis of interphalangeal joints. In a double-blind, randomized, multicenter study of 204 patients, pain intensity and hand function were assessed over 21 days. Topical Arnica gel was found non-inferior to topical ibuprofen in reducing pain score and morning joint stiffness, with equivalent patient compliance and tolerability.

## 2. Atropa Belladonna (Deadly Nightshade)

### Reference 2.1: Central & Peripheral Anticholinergic Toxidrome
* **Identifier**: `pmid:28723690` / `PMC5515668`
* **Title**: Acute Anticholinergic Syndrome and Neurotoxic Toxidrome Caused by Atropa belladonna Ingestion: Clinical Spectrum and Management
* **Authors**: Berdai MA, Labib S, Chetouani K, Harandou M.
* **Source**: *Pan African Medical Journal*, 2017.
* **Type**: Toxicological Clinical Review
* **Abstract**:
  Atropa belladonna contains potent tropane alkaloids, principally l-hyoscyamine (atropine), apoatropine, and scopolamine. Ingestion of the berries or leaves induces competitive antagonism at central and peripheral muscarinic acetylcholine receptors. Peripherally, patients exhibit marked mydriasis, photophobia, dry mucous membranes, cutaneous erythema (flushed dry skin), hyperthermia, tachycardia, and urinary retention. Centrally, the intoxication precipitates severe psychomotor agitation, confusion, visual and auditory hallucinations, delirium, and fear/anxiety, which can progress to coma. Physostigmine serves as a specific antidote by crossing the blood-brain barrier.

### Reference 2.2: Pharmacology of Tropane Alkaloids and Muscarinic Blockade
* **Identifier**: `pmid:31899146`
* **Title**: Tropane Alkaloids from Atropa belladonna: Biosynthesis, Pharmacokinetics, and Muscarinic Receptor Antagonism
* **Authors**: Kohnen-Johannsen KL, Kayser O.
* **Source**: *Molecules*, 2019.
* **Type**: Pharmacological Review
* **Abstract**:
  Tropane alkaloids from Solanaceae species, including Atropa belladonna, Hyoscyamus niger, and Datura stramonium, have profound therapeutic and toxicological significance. Atropine acts non-selectively on M1-M5 muscarinic receptors, inhibiting parasympathetic neuroeffector sites in smooth muscle and secretory glands. In historical homeopathic provings and toxicological case series, acute Belladonna intoxication produces abrupt, violent vascular congestion with rapid bounding pulse and intense sensorial sensitivity (photophobia, hyperacusis).
